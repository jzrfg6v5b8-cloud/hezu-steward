01 / Why this app

Roommate conflict is usually
a record-keeping failure.

Rent, utilities, cleaning, toilet paper, and “we agreed on that” live in chat threads. Hezu turns them into four checkable loops.

01

Bills stay fuzzy

Someone fronts the electric bill. Reimbursement lives in screenshots. Nobody knows the net number.

02

Chores drift

A paper roster on the fridge goes stale. Swaps happen in chat and disappear.

03

Supplies vanish

Shared goods have no stock count. The same person restocks, then quietly resents it.

04

Rules are oral

Quiet hours and guest policy were “mentioned once.” New roommates never signed them.

Product bet: housemates do not need another chat. They need a shared operating system for money, duty, inventory, and consent.

08 / A day in the app

Thirty seconds, not a new chore.

Mia opens Home before leaving. The app tells her what is hers today, what the house is missing, and whether anyone still owes money.

08:10 · Heading out

Home: Ryan on bathroom duty. Tissues low — Ryan buys. She is owed two balances; settle tonight.

19:40 · After groceries

Trash bags hit 3. She taps Consume. Home shows a restock card that can become a Daily bill.

21:15 · Electric arrives

Mia logs $267, split three ways. Ryan and Cole get a confirm card. Net updates; no instant transfer.

Month-end · Close

Two payments only: Ryan $126.50, Cole $84. Mark paid. October archives.

09 / How to demo

Walk the four loops on one house.

Demo home: Sunlight House · Mia Lin, Ryan Zhou, Cole Chen. Start on Home, then Bills → Chores → Stuff → House charter.

Success looks like

Anyone can name their share of a bill. Each zone has an owner this week. At least one supply alerts before it hits zero. The charter has a version and signatures.

Design rules

The house is the only container. Records beat social feed. Show net balances first. Every reminder names who acts.
